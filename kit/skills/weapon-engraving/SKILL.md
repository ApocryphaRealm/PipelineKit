# Weapon engraving - the owner's method

The owner, 2026-11-10 08:17, word for word: *"you take the shape of the material, which is in this case the bit of the
axe, and you trace the inside perimeter of that. You bring it in by a certain amount so that there's a margin between
the engraving and the edge of the weapon, and you take up the entire space within that. You have to make the engraving
fit to that, whether that's tracing an outline with braiding, or you could look up different Norse symbols like Odin's
horns or a Celtic knot or other things."* He called a battleaxe whose carving sat in small framed panels in the middle
of plain steel "trash in terms of the engraving quality and positioning". A panel floating in the middle of a face is
the failure this skill exists to stop.

## The three elements and their jobs (the owner's design rule, 2026-11-10 20:07)

The owner, word for word: *"new design rule, braid goes around something as a perimeter or in a straight line like the
longsword, the weave fills in what the braid goes around and the norse symbol is a centerpiece of the engraving if it
fits the weapon"*. Every engraved face is built from these three, each with ONE job:
- **Braid** - only two uses: a **perimeter** (running round the inset outline of a face or round the centerpiece), or
  a **straight run** along a long narrow field (the greatsword's single central braid). A braid never fills an area and
  never wanders through a field.
- **Weave** (the bold diagonal over-under basket weave of the warhammer) - **fills** whatever a braid goes around.
- **Norse symbol** (triquetra, Odin's horns, valknut...) - the **centerpiece**, set on a **dark filled disc inside a
  polished ring** (the warhammer's look - **the standard**, the owner 2026-11-12 08:05: *"I like the
  symbol with the dark filled circle. So make that the standard"*; this line used to say "warhammer / war axe", but war
  axe v4-v6 had NO disc or ring - its trefoil's outer lobes and dark enclosed spaces only look like one in a crop, and the
  2026-11-12 ring-pass survey passed it by eye; fixed in war axe v7, 2026-11-13), placed in the middle of
  the field (centred as the owner directs, e.g. near the haft but centred up and down on the warhammer), **only if it
  fits the weapon's face** at a bold, readable size. No room - no symbol, the weave fills it.
So a broad face reads: braid perimeter -> weave field -> symbol centerpiece; a long narrow blade reads: one straight
braid. This rule decides the layout; the procedure below decides the measurements.

**A braid "run" must really be straight (the owner, 2026-11-12 00:19, on the bow's curving limb: "it can just be weave since its not straight enough for the braid and theres no symbol for braid to go around").** A curving narrow field with no symbol takes outline + weave, not a braid that bends along it. Refined the same night (00:26, the bow alternate): a braid may follow a CURVED band only when the band is narrow and of even width, so the braid fills it with nothing left over ("it wouldnt be straight but it wouldnt have many areas to fill either"); a curving field of changing width takes outline + weave. The engraving-layout gate measures each run's width variation (and straightness) and fails a run that leaves area to fill.

**Design: outline + weave + symbol, no braid (the owner, 2026-11-11 22:08: "an area can have no braid and just an
outline with weave and the symbol in the center").** A second standard layout beside braid perimeter -> weave ->
symbol, usable on ANY engraved area, not only small ones:
- **Outline** - a flush rim with an incised groove following the face's inset line (no braid strands);
- **Weave** - the bold diagonal over-under weave fills everything inside the outline, strands continuous, mirrored about
  the symbol's axis, every cut cell ending under the outline (no stubs, slivers or corner pieces);
- **Symbol** - the Norse symbol in the middle on the standard dark filled disc and ring (above), on EVERY piece,
  the sheath fittings included (the owner, 2026-11-12 08:35: *"make the symbols all have the black filled ring"* -
  this replaces his 2026-11-11 no-ring choice for the sheath fittings). No room for a bold symbol: outline + weave alone.
Use it where a braid would have nothing to frame or could not pass the symbol without being cut, and whenever the owner
picks it; it is his preferred look for the sheath fittings (below).

**Small fittings (sheath lockets and chapes, and any face too small for a braid to pass its symbol) - the owner,
2026-11-11 22:04-22:06.** On dagger sheath v4 he chose: no ring and no braid round the symbol - the triquetra in the
middle with a plain clear margin, and the rest of the face ONE framed panel of weave (flush rim + groove), mirrored about
the symbol's axis: *"that looks way better, i want to see a similar design on the other sheathes where there's no braid
around the symbol but we use weave with the symbol in the middle"*. Then for the chapes: *"try making the end tips weave
and not braid too"*, and he named the earlier chapes a mistake: a braid round a face with **no centre symbol** has
nothing to go around, which is against the rule. So: **a braid perimeter is used only where it frames something** (a
centerpiece, or a long field it runs along); a face with no symbol is a framed weave panel, not braid + weave.

**Animal engraving (the owner's choice for the kukri dagger, 2026-11-10 20:23: "Jormungandr style engraving that's
swirling around and biting out towards the tip of the blade")** - replaces the braid/weave/symbol layout on a face when
he asks for it:
- one bold serpent body (never thinned to fit - push coils back inside the inset instead), one incised midline, a
  POLISHED raised top on a NEAR-BLACK carved ground (on the dark recipe a wide body otherwise reads as an outline only);
- big loops where the face is widest, clean alternating over-under at crossings;
- bands SOLID with polished tops (a thin band with an incised midline reads as two outlines); no outline-following band
  that runs parallel to the frame line and other strokes ("railroad" lines);
- teeth: place each only where it fits whole inside the face's inset (test against the face itself, not pixels inside a
  hole); draw and reserve the front fang first where an arm tip is short;
- lines that must stay inside the inset (a jawline) are built as an OFFSET of the face's own outline, not hand-placed;
- review cameras on radial parts (mace flanges): within ~60 degrees of the face's plane on its open side, never straight
  down its normal (the neighbour flange covers it);
- the head in PROFILE (one eye, unequal jaws, snout curl, lappet) - a head drawn symmetric about its heading reads as a
  beetle seen from above.

## The procedure (every engraved face)

1. **Take the face's outline.** The region is the whole face of the part being decorated (the axe head's flat, a blade's
   flat, a guard's flat face, a pommel face, a haft panel), as a closed 2D outline in that face's own UV/planar
   coordinates - read from the mesh, not guessed. Exclude the cutting-edge bevel and any edge chamfer: they are not
   part of the face.
2. **Inset it by a margin.** Offset the outline inwards by a margin that stays the same width all round (a true polygon
   offset, not a scale about the centre - scaling gives a thin margin at the narrow end and a fat one at the wide end).
   Margin: about 6-10 % of the face's narrow dimension, never less than ~12 px of the 2K map, so it reads in game.
3. **Fill the whole inset region.** The engraving takes up the entire space inside the inset outline. Nothing is left
   as a plain island the size of a strand or larger, and nothing crosses the inset line.
   - **Border:** the first element is a braid or band that runs along the inset outline itself (offset curves parallel
     to it), following every curve and corner - the shape of the weapon is drawn again in the carving.
   - **Field:** the space inside the border is filled with ONE motif fitted to that space: scaled and bent so it touches
     the border all round, its parts arranged to the region's shape (long regions take a running knot or animal ribbon
     along their length; broad regions take a centred symbol with knotwork filling the corners).
4. **Choose the motif from Norse art** (draw our own version, never copy another game's artwork):
   - Celtic/Norse knotwork: plaits, running knots, interlace that follows the region's axis;
   - Odin's horns (three interlocking drinking horns), the triquetra, the valknut, the triskele;
   - Urnes / Ringerike animal interlace: long serpent ribbons with a head and tail, a few tight knots;
   - for axe heads and broad blades, a symbol at the visual centre (where the region is widest) with knotwork
     radiating to the border.
   Look them up (Wikimedia Commons, museum collections) for their structure; draw them fresh.
5. **Bold enough for 2K and game distance.** Few, thick strands (each ~1/12 - 1/15 of the region's narrow dimension,
   the owner's 08:04 reference), deep dark groove floors, bright polished strand tops (the dark Nordic Steel shading). Maps never above 2048 px.
6. **Check before it is shown.** Render the face straight on with the inset outline drawn over it and report:
   - coverage: the share of the inset region that is carving (strands plus their grooves) - aim above ~85 %;
   - margin: the smallest and largest margin width measured all round - they must be within ~25 % of each other;
   - nothing crosses the inset line.
   Put the numbers on the sheet. A face that fails is fixed before the owner sees it.
7. **Check the surface is level before it is carved** (the owner, 2026-11-12 02:55: *"add a gate and a skill update to
   check for surface elevation"*). The face the engraving sits on must be one even surface - either FLAT or UNIFORMLY
   ROUNDED (02:58: *"consistent even rounding of a carving like with the pommel as if the surface was rounded uniformly
   in the bow pics before it would have been a different story"*). Whatever its shape, it is consistent: the same
   cross-section profile (crown height and curvature) all along the face. A curved part (a bow's band and hook) keeps a
   constant thickness and crown along its length, and an emblem or medallion keeps one even height instead of following
   the taper of the part it sits on (greatsword v3 -> v4, 2026-11-11). Rounding is fine; rounding that changes along the
   face is not. The
   relief is then carved to a constant depth into that surface. A face that rises, dips or twists shows as a highlight
   wandering across it under a raking light - the owner sees it at once (bow v7's hook, greatsword v3's emblem). So:
   - take cross-sections across each carved face at even steps along its length, fit each one's crown height and
     curvature, and measure the residual against the fitted uniform surface (a plane, or the constant profile swept
     along the face); put the spread of crown and curvature along the face, and the residual, on the sheet;
   - render each carved face under a raking light at the owner's camera and look at the highlight - it must run
     evenly, not band or wander;
   - the surface-elevation gate refuses the render when a face fails (see "Gates" below). The layout gate's
     "fragments 0" says nothing about this - both are needed.

## Learned on the set (2026-11-10)

- **Bold proportion:** a strand reads bold only at about half the band's half width (the approved A5d sword). Strands
  under ~0.2 units read as fine rope hatching in close-ups. The gap where one strand passes under another scales with
  the strand width - a fixed gap on thin strands broke the braid into dashes.
- **Perimeter braids** need strands of at least ~0.25 units (thinner read as rope hatching in the front view even at
  60 px/unit), and are sized from the face's NARROWEST flat - otherwise the two runs clash down the middle of a narrow
  root (dagger v2).
- **Mixed texel densities:** an atlas holding parts at different px-per-unit needs the normal map computed per part at
  its own density - one value for all halved the guards' normal strength on earlier sheets.
- **Faces of mixed width** (broad in one place, ~2 units elsewhere - a bow riser): split the face into ZONES first; the
  broad zone takes braid perimeter + weave + symbol, each narrow arm one straight braid. A perimeter run round the whole
  mixed face read as rope hatching. A straight braid takes its width only from centre-line points inside its own zone.
- **Curls 1.0-1.4 wide:** a two-strand braid reads as interlace; three strands read as dashes.
- **Tone on big faces:** the weave's polished strand tops make a large flat face read bright silver head-on; judge the
  set's dark tone on the three-quarter view and say so on the sheet (or tone the front light down for the standard views).
- **Margins of a narrow part** are measured against that part's own edge, never the whole outline.
- **Hidden parts:** cut the parts of a face under a collar or sleeve out of the face BEFORE insetting, or the margin and
  braid start under the collar.
- **Narrow faces** (a guard arm 2-3 units across): there is no room for a border band AND a field at bold size. Fill the
  whole inset with ONE bold running knot whose edges follow the inset outline and narrow into the ends - state it on the
  sheet as the narrow-face case.
- **Symbols read as faces:** a triquetra with two lobes up inside a ring read as a skull on a round pommel. Turn it one
  lobe up, and look at every centred symbol at game distance for an unintended face.
- **Sharp tips:** the last part of a sharp point narrower than two margins has no inset - it stays plain polished steel;
  measure margins excluding that zone and say so.

## Bladed weapons: blank first, one bevel, the rest is the engraving area (the owner, 2026-11-15, katana A)

The owner, 16:45: "start with a blank katana with no engraving ... two flat sides with no bevel, you cut out the blade
shape that we have, then you define the edge bevel, and there's only one edge bevel with a consistent angle from the
handle all the way up to the tip. And then whatever's not the bevel is the engraving area ... the part towards the tip
where the engraving area curves toward the spine should be the same curve and angle as the edge itself, so that they
are consistent and parallel." And 16:35: "there needs to be a consistent margin for the edge bevel and the braid cant go
into that margin"; 16:39: "the bladed weapons should have an edge where both sides come to a point - the edge which has a
bevel".

Build every blade in this order, and show the blank before carving it if the shape is in question:
1. **Outline** - spine and edge lines meeting at a sharp point (no flat-topped tip).
2. **Two flat faces** cut to that outline.
3. **ONE edge bevel** - a band of constant width measured square to the edge, all along it, from the flat down to a sharp
   edge where both faces meet. Constant width at constant thickness = the same angle everywhere. Its inner line is the
   edge offset by that width (smooth the offset line - unevenly sampled points drew a jagged border), so through the tip
   it runs parallel to the edge's curve until it meets the spine.
4. **The engraving area = everything that is not bevel**: from the spine to the bevel's inner line, ending in a point on
   the spine. Its border margin is the same width all round; the carving never enters it.
5. **Carve the area**: an end symbol (triquetra) in its own cell at the base, closed by a groove line the braid's strands
   tuck under; then the braid up the area, ending ON a crossing short of the point, plain steel past it.

Learned getting there (each cost a round with the owner):
- **A rough drawn line is intent, not geometry** - "use your head, it was just a rough line, make it look good".
- **Changing one thing changes only that thing.** "Use a finer braid" means a smaller crossing pitch - not a narrower field.
  Painting the whole run on one map lowered its texel density, and the painter's px-scaled border doubled and shrank the
  field. Fix the border in world units when the density changes.
- **One continuous braid, no seam halfway** - the owner saw the old two-panel break. Size the texel density so the whole
  run fits one map.
- **The dark ground must not run on past the last strand** (a "black spot" at the tip). End the braid on a crossing and
  make everything past it, inside the groove, plain steel - find that point from the braid's phase, then trim back past
  the last half crossing; a "last strand pixel" test is fooled by stray slivers.
- **Polished flat steel facing the camera renders black** (it mirrors the dark ground) - a plain end reads as a hole.
  Brush it like the strand tops.
- **A clear gap beside the braid cannot be painted dark at this scale**: the clean-up fills any pocket that thin, and the
  braid's ground turns light. Keep the set's convention - strands tuck under the border groove - and keep the BEVEL margin
  (outside the groove) even and untouched.
- **A small field gets a small symbol, in its own cell**: at about 2 units across, the triquetra fits at R ~0.68 with
  0.11 strands; the painter's bold-symbol minimum (R 0.9) does not apply to an end cell. Its enclosed spaces are dark by
  design - exempt them from the fragment count, as the medallion code does.

Tools: in the original a blade builder and a face painter of its design workspace did this (not in the kit; build the
faces with `scripts\art\patternlib` - role `patternlib-cli`). The painter's straight-face opt-ins, per face: `braid_pitch` (finer = smaller), `braid_margin` (border in world units), `braid_min_hw`,
`braid_end_at_crossing` + `braid_end_phase` + `braid_end_trim`, `plain_end_brushed`, `end_symbol` [x, y, R, sw].

## Tracing the owner's drawn marks (2026-11-10, battleaxe v6/v8, warhammer v2)

- **Symmetry overrides a literal trace** (the owner, 2026-11-10 19:21: *"my line was followed too literally, ignoring
  the rules of symmetry"* - the warhammer head came out wider on one side of the haft than the other). His lines are
  followed exactly for the CURVES he drew, but a weapon's natural symmetries hold unless he says otherwise:
  the head is as tall on both sides where it meets the haft and centred on it; the front and back faces mirror;
  paired parts (two guard arms, two crescent tips meant as a pair, both edges of a double-edged blade) match; a part
  that sits on the weapon's axis stays on it. Where his line would break one of these, keep the symmetry, blend his
  curve into it over a short distance, and state on the sheet where and by how much. When it is unclear whether an
  asymmetry is intended, ask in one line before building.
  **Caution (19:31, the same day):** "wider on one side than the other" turned out to mean the HAMMER side was out of
  proportion (it should widen gradually toward the striking face, as in v1) - not that the two sides must meet the haft
  at equal heights; the "symmetry fix" then wrongly moved the pick. A hammer and a pick are different parts and need not
  match. Before applying symmetry, say back in one line which parts you will make match and which you will leave alone.
- Register his screenshot onto our render by the weapon's OUTLINE, not grey levels (he often draws on an older,
  differently engraved render), and take the backdrop colour from a known backdrop pixel - an image corner can be a
  dark UI band and gave two wrong alignments.
- Split his strokes into separate pieces BEFORE any morphological closing - a closing merged two strokes where they
  nearly touched at a tip.
- His lines follow the FRONT FACE's edges, not the outer silhouette: measure the overlay against the rendered front-face
  edge (measured against the silhouette, a thick hammer slab "failed" by ~7 %).
- A pen stroke hooks in its last few pixels: when a line must be carried on to meet a part, take its direction over the
  last ~0.8 units, not the last few points; say on the sheet wherever a line was extended or joined.
- An outline the owner drew as ONE curve stays one curve: fit a single smooth curve over its whole length (no blend
  between two lines - a short blend gave a 20 degree slope swing over 1.5 units, a visible kink) and put its slope at
  the ends and at each part it crosses on the sheet. His earlier still-standing marks are MUST-KEEP constraints in every
  later order, and every overlay re-checks all of them.
- **Classify every mark before building** (the owner, 2026-11-10 23:04, after the shield was built backwards: "I
  literally drew the lines for what you should fill in with metal ... I just drew triangles. I didn't want them to be
  actual triangles. I just meant that as a positional reference"):
  - an OUTLINE to follow exactly (a line along or across an existing edge);
  - an AREA to fill (a line drawn over empty space next to a part - on the shield, the green circle over the black board
    meant "metal out to here", NOT a limit to trim to);
  - a MARKER for position only (a simple symbol - triangle, X, arrow - over an existing feature: keep the feature's own
    shape and move it to the marker).
  When it is not obvious, ASK it as a question with the options and wait - a reading told to him is not his consent.
  Hand-drawn closed shapes touching open strokes separate cleanly as enclosed holes (no morphology).
- **Circular rims:** where a part meets a round rim, its edge follows a circle concentric with the rim, at an even gap
  (the owner, 22:47: "perfect circles ... a consistent gap between them"); openings and gaps between parts stay.
- Band widths stay constant along a run; a band whose width changes pixel by pixel came out wavy and hollow - let a
  frame line take up the difference instead.

- **His marks are a rough guide, not a blueprint** (the owner, 2026-11-12 02:35, bow v5: *"i said it was a rough
  outline not exact and to make sure it looks right"*). Build a markup as PATCHES onto the existing outline: replace
  only the regions his marks change and keep every unmarked region's geometry exactly as it was. Bow v5 rebuilt the
  whole riser outline by tracing all his green lines, so the plate bottom and the inner panel - neither of which he
  asked to change - came out as traces of his freehand line, with a step where the trace met the old collar end; it
  took v6 and v7 to get v4's plate bottom back. Where a rough line disagrees with a shape he did not ask to change,
  keep the existing shape.
- **Recover the camera of the picture he drew on** before building from it (bow v4 -> v5): find which render it is,
  fit its camera, map his pixels into model coordinates, and prove the result by laying his lines over a render from
  that camera.
- **A join between two outlines must be faired** (bow v7's right edge, the owner 02:51: "smoothe that out"): blending
  old geometry back in over a short distance left a bulge and dip. Fair the whole edge between the two fixed ends and
  check its curvature does not change sign where it should not.

## Gates (run at design time; a render is refused when one fails)

- **engraving-layout gate** (`scripts\gates\engraving-layout-gate.py`, via the design workspace's gate chain (the original's design-workspace scripts, not in the kit)): layout
  records, fragments, crossings, margins, braid runs (straight, even-width, or a smooth taper to a point - width slope
  at most 0.35, leftover at most 0.10; a run the owner asked for by name carries his words as `owner_ruling` and only
  warns). Since 2026-11-12 a weave field FAILs when its motif is too coarse for its panel: room for under 24 motif
  pieces (bow v5's plate had 18, every piece cut by the frame, and read as blocky T and H shapes).
- **medallion check** (in the engraving-layout gate, 2026-11-13, after the war axe shipped a bare triquetra that a
  by-eye survey had passed): every face whose layout names a centerpiece symbol must carry a MEASUREMENT of its maps in
  `<work>\medallion_check.json`, written by a medallion measure of the maps (the original's design-workspace scripts, not in the kit; the fields are
  the ones `scripts\gates\engraving-layout-gate.py` reads). The measure finds the circle round the symbol where carving floor steps up to flush steel and fails when there is none (no disc /
  no ring), the dark band inside it is under 2 px or brighter than 0.25 x the steel, or the flush ring is under 2 px,
  under 3 x the floor's brightness or not 0.2 smoother. A record with `"ring": null` fails too. Unmeasured: warns at
  design time, FAILS at packaging. Never judge "it has the disc and ring" from a crop - measure it.
- **surface-profile gate** (`scripts\gates\surface-profile-gate.py`, role `surface-profile-gate`, 2026-11-12, the
  owner's order above; run after the layout gate). Measured (by the original's design-workspace scripts, not in the kit)
  in Blender, on the exported NIF, then analysed in system Python into `<work>\surface_profile.json`.
  It judges the surface the GAME SHADES - the interpolated vertex normals - not only the geometry: each carved face is
  cut into a cross-section every 0.2 along its centre line and each section's normal lean is fitted as
  tilt + crown x s. FAIL when, along the face: crown spread (p95-p5) > 5 deg (rounding that changes), tilt spread
  > 5 deg (leaning in and out, twist), section residual p95 > 1.5 deg (bumps), detrended lean along the centre line
  > 1.5 deg (rises and falls). Flat = crown 0; evenly rounded = the same crown all along - both pass.
  Calibration: fails bow v7's band and hook (25 / 19 / 6.4) and greatsword v3's emblem face; passes bow v8 (0.3 / 0.6 /
  0.1) and the greatsword's gently curved blade flats. `TN_SKIP_SURFACE_GATE=1` only for deliberate old-version
  comparisons.
- **Why a flat face can still shade uneven** (bow v8, 2026-11-12): bow v7's riser faces were flat to 0.0000 in shape,
  but each face shared its outline vertices with the 48 deg polished chamfer and had no inner vertices, so the NIF's
  averaged normals leaned ~24 deg and the face shaded like a pillow - on a narrow band the highlight wandered. Give
  every flat carved face its own vertices (split from the chamfer) so its normals stay true. Every slab-built part
  of the set carried this (bow v2-v7, greatsword guards, pommel discs) until fixed.

## Every sheet shown to the owner

The owner, 2026-11-10 19:22: *"from now on give me 3 standard views, front, side and top views."* Every weapon sheet
leads with three ORTHOGRAPHIC views of each version, same scale, no perspective: **front** (the flat face), **side**
(along the edge or spine - thickness and taper), **top** (down the long axis - thickness and symmetry about the haft).
Close-ups, the overlay with his marks, the engraving check and the game-distance view follow.

- Any outline the owner draws is traced from the pixels and checked with an overlay (past-mistakes library, "Followed
  the owner's drawn outline while the result did not match it").
